Modern Maize Cultivation in Pakistan: Complete Production Guide for 2026

July 2026 Crop Guide 14 min read

Maize is Pakistan's third most important cereal crop with tremendous growth potential. This comprehensive guide provides modern farming techniques to help you achieve 40-60 bags (2400-3600 kg) per acre yield through proper hybrid selection, timely planting, scientific irrigation, and integrated pest management.

Why Maize Matters in Pakistan Agriculture

Maize production in Pakistan has grown significantly over the last two decades. With rising demand from the poultry and feed industries, as well as food applications, maize represents an excellent opportunity for Pakistani farmers to increase income. Modern farming practices combined with improved hybrids can double or triple traditional yields.

Maize requires careful planning and execution throughout its lifecycle, from seed selection to harvest. This guide covers every step to ensure you maximize your investment and achieve optimal returns.

1. Selecting the Right Maize Hybrid

Hybrid selection is crucial as it determines your crop's potential yield, resistance to diseases, and adaptation to local conditions.

Popular High-Yield Hybrids in Pakistan:

  • Pioneer P-30G95: High yield potential 50-60 bags/acre, excellent grain quality, resistant to common diseases. Best for irrigated areas.
  • Syngenta NK-40: Good for both summer and spring planting, yields 45-55 bags/acre, strong root system for deep soils.
  • Monsanto DK-7018: Disease-tolerant, 40-50 bags/acre, suitable for farmers without irrigation.
  • FFC-900: Pakistani hybrid, well-adapted to local conditions, yields 35-45 bags/acre, economical option.
  • Advanta 30Y87: Drought-tolerant, good for rainfed areas, medium maturity (90-95 days).

Key Selection Criteria:

  • Choose hybrids with proven performance in your district
  • Consider maturity period based on your crop rotation
  • Select varieties with disease resistance to local problems
  • Verify seed certification from authorized dealers
  • Check seed viability and germination rates

Always purchase certified hybrid seed from authorized dealers to ensure purity and germination rates above 85%.

2. Understanding Maize Climate and Season

Maize can be grown in multiple seasons in Pakistan with proper planning.

Growing Seasons:

  • Spring/Kharif Maize (April-May planting): Harvested in August-September. Highest yielding season with good rainfall. Most farmers prefer this season.
  • Summer Maize (June-July planting): Planted after spring crops, harvested in November-December. Requires careful irrigation planning.
  • Winter Maize (December planting): Grown in warm regions of Sindh and South Punjab, harvested in April-May.

Climate Requirements:

  • Optimal temperature: 20-30°C during growing season
  • Rainfall: 500-700 mm annually (supplemented by irrigation)
  • Relative humidity: 40-60%
  • Sunshine: 150-200 hours per month for maximum productivity

3. Soil Preparation and Land Management

Quality soil preparation forms the foundation of high-yield maize farming.

Ideal Soil Conditions:

  • Sandy-loam to clay-loam texture
  • Soil pH: 6.0-7.0 (neutral soil preferred)
  • Organic matter: 2-3%
  • Good drainage and aeration

Pre-Planting Land Preparation:

  1. Stubble management: Remove and destroy previous crop residues
  2. Ploughing: Perform 2-3 deep ploughings 15-20 days apart
  3. Harrowing: Use harrow to achieve fine soil tilth
  4. Organic matter: Incorporate 8-10 tons of well-rotted farm manure per acre
  5. Leveling: Ensure proper leveling for uniform irrigation
  6. Bed preparation: Create 60-75 cm wide planting beds

4. Seed Rate and Sowing Technique

Proper spacing and plant population are critical for maximizing yield potential.

Recommended Plant Population:

  • Irrigated areas: 65,000-70,000 plants per acre
  • Rainfed areas: 50,000-60,000 plants per acre
  • Seed rate: 13-14 kg per acre (for 85% germination)
  • Row spacing: 60-75 cm
  • Plant-to-plant spacing: 20-25 cm

Sowing Methods:

  • Manual dibbling: Traditional method, labor-intensive but accurate placement
  • Mechanical planter: Saves time and labor, ensures uniform spacing
  • Broadcasting: Not recommended as it leads to uneven emergence

Seed Treatment:

Before planting, treat seeds with fungicide (Thiram 3g/kg) and insecticide (Imidacloprid 600FS @ 2 ml/kg) to protect against soil-borne diseases and early infestation.

5. Nutrition Management for Maximum Yield

Maize is a heavy feeder requiring proper nutrient management for high productivity.

Recommended Nutrient Application (per acre):

Nutrient Target Yield (40 bags) Target Yield (60 bags) Application Method
Nitrogen (N) 100-120 kg 150-180 kg Basal 40%, 30% at V4, 30% at V8-V10
Phosphorus (P) 60-80 kg 80-100 kg 100% basal application
Potassium (K) 40-50 kg 60-80 kg 50% basal, 50% at tasseling
Zinc (Zn) 5 kg 5 kg Basal if deficiency evident

Fertilizer Application Strategy:

Basal Application (at planting): Mix all phosphorus, potassium, and half the nitrogen with farm manure and apply in the seed furrows.

First Top Dressing (30-35 days, V4 stage): Apply 30% nitrogen using urea when plants have 4 true leaves.

Second Top Dressing (50-60 days, V8-V10 stage): Apply remaining 30% nitrogen at vegetative stage for ear development.

Tip: Fertigation (applying fertilizer through irrigation) improves efficiency and allows split applications.

6. Water Management and Irrigation Schedule

Proper irrigation timing and quantity are crucial for high maize yields.

Critical Water Requirement Stages:

  • Germination stage (0-15 days): Light irrigation to maintain soil moisture
  • Vegetative stage (15-45 days): 50-75 mm water every 10-15 days
  • Critical stage (45-90 days, V6-tassel): 75-100 mm every 7-10 days (most critical period)
  • Grain filling stage (90-120 days): 50-75 mm every 15-20 days
  • Maturity stage (120+ days): Reduced irrigation frequency

Total Water Requirement:

  • Irrigated maize: 20-24 irrigations, 600-750 mm total
  • Semi-arid areas: 12-16 irrigations, 400-500 mm total
  • Rainfed: Depends on rainfall pattern

Key Point: Avoid waterlogging as it promotes root diseases. Ensure proper drainage between irrigations.

7. Pest Management in Maize

Integrated pest management (IPM) reduces losses while minimizing pesticide use.

Major Pests and Control Measures:

Stem Borer (most damaging):

  • Symptoms: Circular holes in leaves, dead hearts in young plants, broken stems
  • Control: Use Bt hybrids, remove and destroy affected plants, spray Chlorpyrifos 20% EC (500 ml/acre) at 30-35 days
  • Biological: Encourage parasitoid wasps, use pheromone traps

Armyworm:

  • Symptoms: Ragged holes in leaves, visible larvae in whorls
  • Control: Scout regularly, spray Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) when 10-15% plants are affected
  • Chemical: Spinosad 45% SC (100 ml/acre) for severe infestations

Fall Armyworm (emerging threat):

  • Symptoms: Irregular holes starting from leaf margins, appear suddenly
  • Control: Early detection crucial, spray Fipronil 5% SC (250 ml/acre) at first sign
  • Prevention: Crop rotation, weed management, field sanitation

Cutworm Management:

  • Use barriers around seedlings in nurseries
  • Apply granulated insecticides (Malathion 5% @ 10 kg/acre) in furrows at planting
  • Plow fields before planting to expose pupae

8. Disease Management

Early identification and management of diseases prevents significant yield loss.

Common Maize Diseases in Pakistan:

Leaf Blotch:

  • Cause: Fungal (Bipolaris species)
  • Management: Use resistant varieties, spray Mancozeb 75% WP (600 g/acre) at V8 stage

Stem Rot/Anthracnose:

  • Cause: Colletotrichum species
  • Management: Improve drainage, use disease-free seed, destroy affected plants

Rust:

  • Cause: Puccinia species
  • Management: Not economically significant in Pakistan, resistant varieties available

9. Weed Management Strategy

Effective weed control can increase yields by 20-30%.

Integrated Weed Management Program:

  • Pre-planting: Remove weeds during land preparation
  • Cultural practices: Proper spacing, timely irrigation, crop rotation
  • Chemical control: Atrazine 50% WP (1 kg/acre) or Paraquat (500 ml/acre) at 25-30 days
  • Manual weeding: At 45 and 60 days after emergence
  • Mulching: Use maize stover or plastic to suppress weeds

Critical period: First 30-45 days after emergence - most critical for weed control

10. Harvesting and Yield Maximization

Proper harvesting timing and technique preserve grain quality.

Harvest Indicators:

  • Moisture content: 18-22%
  • Grain color: Healthy grain color (yellow/white depending on variety)
  • Days to maturity: 100-110 days from planting
  • Cob color: Black cob indicates complete maturity

Harvesting Process:

  1. Use mechanical picker or hand harvesting for small fields
  2. Separate cob from grain using thresher
  3. Dry grain to 13-14% moisture for storage
  4. Clean and grade grain for market sale

Expected Yields with Good Management:

  • Traditional practices: 15-20 bags per acre (900-1200 kg)
  • Good management: 40-50 bags per acre (2400-3000 kg)
  • Excellent management: 55-60+ bags per acre (3300-3600+ kg)

11. Cost and Profitability Analysis

Understanding your costs helps maximize profitability.

Production Cost Breakdown (per acre):

  • Land preparation: 6,000-8,000 PKR
  • Hybrid seed: 8,000-12,000 PKR
  • Fertilizers: 25,000-35,000 PKR
  • Pesticides/herbicides: 8,000-12,000 PKR
  • Irrigation: 15,000-20,000 PKR
  • Labor: 25,000-30,000 PKR
  • Harvesting/transport: 15,000-20,000 PKR
  • Miscellaneous: 5,000-8,000 PKR
  • TOTAL COST: 107,000-145,000 PKR

Revenue Calculation (50 bags/acre yield):

  • Yield: 50 bags (3000 kg)
  • Average price: 3000-3500 PKR per bag
  • Gross revenue: 150,000-175,000 PKR
  • Net profit: 30,000-68,000 PKR per acre

Profitability varies based on market prices, yield achievement, and management efficiency. Better management practices consistently improve net returns.

Conclusion

Modern maize farming in Pakistan offers significant income potential when farmers adopt scientific practices. The combination of quality hybrid seeds, precise nutrition management, timely irrigation, and integrated pest management can nearly triple yields compared to traditional farming. Start implementing these recommendations gradually, monitor results, and adjust based on local conditions and market feedback.
