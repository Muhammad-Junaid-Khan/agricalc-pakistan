Complete Guide to Sugarcane Farming in Pakistan: Best Practices for 2026

July 2026 Crop Guide 12 min read

Sugarcane is one of Pakistan's most important cash crops, contributing significantly to the agricultural economy. This comprehensive guide covers everything from selecting the right variety to harvesting at peak yield, with practical tips based on proven farming methods.

Introduction to Sugarcane Farming

Sugarcane cultivation has been a cornerstone of Pakistan's agriculture for decades. With the right knowledge and techniques, farmers can significantly improve their yields and profitability. This guide provides evidence-based recommendations for successful sugarcane farming across Pakistan's diverse climate zones.

1. Choosing the Right Sugarcane Variety

Selecting the appropriate variety is the foundation of successful sugarcane farming. Different varieties perform better in different regions and climates.

Popular Varieties in Pakistan:

  • CP-43-33: High sugar content, excellent for crushing, matures in 10-12 months. Suitable for Sindh and Punjab plains.
  • S-2003: Disease-resistant, good ratoon yield, suitable for cooler climates in northern areas.
  • CPF-237: High fiber content, excellent for bagasse production, grows well in diverse soil conditions.
  • TN-95-31: High productivity, suitable for newly developed agricultural zones.
  • S-2002: Moderate height, disease-tolerant, recommended for small landholdings.

Consult with your local agricultural extension office to determine the best variety for your specific region and soil type.

2. Land Preparation and Soil Requirements

Sugarcane requires well-prepared land to establish strong root systems and achieve optimal growth.

Soil Characteristics:

  • Well-drained loamy or clay-loam soil
  • Soil pH between 6.0 to 7.5
  • Good organic matter content (2-3%)
  • Avoid waterlogged areas

Field Preparation Steps:

  1. Clear previous crop residue thoroughly
  2. Perform 3-4 deep ploughings with 15-20 days interval
  3. Apply 15-20 tons of well-rotted farm manure per acre
  4. Create furrows 75 cm apart for planting
  5. Ensure proper water drainage channels

3. Planting and Propagation

Proper planting techniques significantly influence germination rates and early crop growth.

Planting Time:

Spring planting (February-March): Recommended for areas with cool winters and warm summers. This allows maximum growth period before harvest.

Autumn planting (October-November): Suitable for areas with mild winters. Gives crops time to establish before summer.

Seed Requirements:

  • Use disease-free seed material from certified nurseries
  • Seed rate: 40-50 quintals (4000-5000 kg) per acre
  • Use setts with 2-3 buds for better germination
  • Treat seeds with fungicide (Thiram 2g/liter) to prevent rotting

Planting Technique:

Place setts in furrows at 20-25 cm spacing. Cover with soil and water immediately after planting. Proper depth (5-6 cm) is crucial for uniform germination.

4. Irrigation Management

Water management is critical for achieving high sugarcane yields. Sugarcane requires 45-50 irrigations annually, depending on rainfall and climate.

Irrigation Schedule:

Growth Stage Frequency (Days) Water Depth (mm)
Initial (0-60 days) 15-20 50-60
Tillering (60-150 days) 10-15 60-75
Grand Growth (150-270 days) 7-10 75-100
Maturation (270+ days) 15-20 50-60

Avoid waterlogging as it promotes fungal diseases and root rot. Proper drainage between irrigation cycles is essential.

5. Fertilizer Application

Sugarcane is a heavy feeder requiring substantial nutrient inputs for optimal growth and sugar accumulation.

Recommended Fertilizer Dose (per acre):

  • Nitrogen (N): 150-200 kg
  • Phosphorus (P): 80-100 kg
  • Potassium (K): 80-100 kg
  • Zinc (Zn): 5-10 kg (if deficiency exists)

Application Timing:

Basal Dose (at planting): 50% nitrogen, full phosphorus and potassium, all micronutrients

First Top Dressing (45-60 days): 25% nitrogen

Second Top Dressing (90-120 days): 25% nitrogen

6. Pest and Disease Management

Early detection and management of pests and diseases can prevent significant crop losses.

Common Sugarcane Pests:

  • Sugarcane Borers: Use pheromone traps, parasitoid wasps, or chemical control (Chlorpyrifos 20% EC)
  • Stem Borer: Remove infested stalks, use bio-control agents
  • Leafhopper: Spray with Imidacloprid 17.8% SL at 100 ml per acre

Major Diseases:

  • Red Rot: Use resistant varieties, practice crop rotation, disinfect seed material
  • Smut: Treat seed with hot water (52°C for 2 hours) before planting
  • Wilt: Use disease-free seeds, ensure good drainage

7. Weed Management

Weeds compete with sugarcane for nutrients, water, and light. Effective weed control can increase yields by 20-30%.

Integrated Weed Management:

  • Two manual weedings at 60 and 90 days after planting
  • Use herbicides: 2,4-D (1.5 liters/acre) at 30-40 days after planting
  • Mulching with sugarcane trash or other organic materials
  • Avoid flooding which favors weed growth

8. Harvesting and Yield Optimization

Timing is critical for harvesting sugarcane to achieve maximum sugar content and yield.

Harvest Indicators:

  • Crop maturity: 10-12 months after planting (varies by variety)
  • Leaves start yellowing and drying
  • Internodes swell and sugar concentration increases
  • Brix reading: 18-20% indicates optimal harvest time

Harvesting Best Practices:

  • Cut stalks at ground level (5-10 cm above soil)
  • Remove tops and trash before transport
  • Minimize delay between cutting and crushing
  • Expected yield: 40-50 tons per acre (varies by variety and conditions)

9. Ratoon Crop Management

Ratoon crops (subsequent crops from stubble) can produce 2-3 additional harvests before replanting.

Ratoon Management Steps:

  • Remove trash and dead leaves after harvesting
  • Perform gap filling for missing plants
  • Apply similar fertilizer schedule as plant crop
  • Irrigate immediately after harvest for early sprouting
  • Ratoon yields typically 20-30% lower than plant crop

10. Cost Analysis and Profitability

Understanding production costs helps farmers make informed decisions and improve profitability.

Typical Cost Breakdown (per acre):

  • Land preparation: 8,000-10,000 PKR
  • Seed and planting: 15,000-20,000 PKR
  • Fertilizers: 35,000-45,000 PKR
  • Irrigation: 25,000-35,000 PKR
  • Labor and management: 40,000-50,000 PKR
  • Harvesting and transport: 30,000-40,000 PKR
  • Total cost: ~180,000-240,000 PKR

Expected Revenue:

  • Yield: 40-50 tons per acre
  • Market price: 4,500-6,000 PKR per ton
  • Gross revenue: 180,000-300,000 PKR per acre
  • Net profit: 50,000-100,000 PKR per acre

These figures are approximate and vary based on market conditions, farm management practices, and regional factors.

Conclusion

Successful sugarcane farming requires attention to multiple factors including variety selection, soil preparation, irrigation management, proper fertilization, and pest control. By following these evidence-based practices, Pakistani farmers can significantly improve their sugarcane yields and profitability. Stay updated with your local agricultural extension office for region-specific guidance and market information.
